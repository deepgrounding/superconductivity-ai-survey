## Substantive

### 2. The headline contrast compares a 200 GPa prediction with an ambient-pressure measurement

The abstract: "recent screens advertise candidates above 200 K, while the one closed
prediction-synthesis-measurement loop in the recent literature delivered
superconductors between 2.5 and 6.5 K." The conclusion repeats it, calling it "the
survey's most concrete result."

Both numbers are individually correct. arXiv:2502.16558 reports 144 candidates above
200 K, and arXiv:2604.23758 reports Zr₃ScRe₈ at 6.5 K, HfZrRe₄ at 5.9 K, Zr₄VRe₇ at
3.5 K and Hf₂₁Re₂₅ at 2.5 K. But the 200 K figure is **at 200 GPa**, and the measured
2.5–6.5 K compounds are ambient-pressure intermetallics. §5 states the pressure
("144 candidates above 200 K at 200 GPa"); the abstract, the §5 assessment and the
conclusion all drop it.

As written, a reader takes the gap to be prediction-versus-measurement when part of
it is megabar-versus-ambient. The paper's own argument does not need the elision —
the hydride screens are unsynthesised at any pressure, which is the point. Six words
in three places fixes it.

### 3. The validation figures cannot be reproduced from the released artifacts

`llm_label.py --benchmark` issues **fresh API calls** against `label_truth.csv`
rather than scoring the labels written into the corpus. The reported figures
therefore come from a run that no longer exists. Both runs use the final prompt —
synthesis recall matches at 64% — so this is run-to-run variation, not a
prompt-version mismatch.

Re-scoring the shipped `llm_labels.jsonl` against `label_truth.csv`, 146 rows after
the same bleed exclusion the script uses (confirmed: the `family_true` and
`task_true` bleed filters select the same four rows):

| metric | paper reports | released labels | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| family agreement | 85.6% | 86.3% (126/146) | 1 paper |
| mode-of-inquiry agreement | 88.4% | 87.0% (127/146) | 2 papers |
| recall, theory | 96% | 100% (57/57) | 2 papers |
| recall, characterization | 92% | 90% (47/52) | 1 paper |
| recall, ab initio | 92% | 85% (11/13) | 1 paper |
| recall, synthesis | 64% | 64% (7/11) | 0 |
| recall, discovery | 54% | 38% (5/13) | 2 papers |

No number here is wrong by more than two papers, and the paper already discloses
that the two small classes are weak. The finding is not that the classifier is worse
than claimed. It is that **a reader who re-scores the released labels gets different
numbers than the paper reports**, in a paper whose central methodological claim is
that its taxonomy was tested rather than asserted.

The swing is worth reporting in its own right: two papers is ±15 points on n=11 and
n=13, which tells the reader how little a 13-item recall estimate constrains
anything. That is a more honest characterisation of the small classes than "the two
small classes are the weak ones."

Fix: add a mode that scores `llm_labels.jsonl` against `label_truth.csv` and report
those numbers, with the per-class figures as fractions rather than percentages.
They are the ones the corpus rests on and the ones a reader can reproduce.

### 4. Four records are not labelled the way the Method section says they are

Four rows carry `label_source == 'rule'` and an empty `off_topic`:

    2204.02214  Stable pair liquid phase in fermionic systems
    2305.10039  High orbital-moment Cooper pairs by crystalline symmetry breaking
    2505.08898  Probing time-reversal symmetry breaking at microwave frequencies
    2606.05210  Superconducting Triple Point in UTe2: Thermodynamics and Symmetry

The Method section says both axes and the AI lens "were assigned by a language-model
pass over title and abstract". For these four it was the keyword rules, which the
same section reports as inadequate. That is a provenance mismatch, not a counting
error: the stated arithmetic (10,303 − 254 = 10,049) is correct, and a record never
flagged is genuinely not excluded under that definition.

Worth noting that counting strictly on `off_topic == False` gives 10,245 and 10,045,
and that the figure appears in the title, the abstract, Table 1's total row, §6, the
conclusion and inside Figure 1's rendered artwork — so if the four come back
off-topic, the fix reaches the figure, not just the prose. Re-running four records
costs nothing and makes the description true.

## Editorial

### 5. Hypothesis 3 asserts something the corpus cannot show

"Institutional role assignment" states as fact that AI-tagged authors "are frequently
from computational or machine-learning-methods groups rather than from the synthesis
and spectroscopy groups". The corpus has an `authors` column and no affiliations, so
nothing released supports it. The paragraph's closing disclaimer covers the
hypothesis, not this antecedent. Affiliations are recoverable from arXiv metadata for
253 rows if you want the claim; otherwise mark the sentence as impression.

### 6. The recommendations are stated six times

Release characterization data, build a time-stamped benchmark, model
synthesizability — in §6 "What the ranking implies" (3 items), §6 "Assessment" (3
clauses), §7 "Four things that would have to be built" (4), §7 "The honest statement
of the route" (4 numbered steps), §8 "What would change the assessment" (3), and the
conclusion (3). The cardinality drifts between three and four, so a reader cannot
tell which is the paper's list. Merging §7's two lists and cutting §8's restatement
would sharpen the close at no cost.
