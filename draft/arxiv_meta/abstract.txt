Superconductivity research generates enormous quantities of data and has a poor record of predicting its own discoveries, which makes it an obvious target for AI for Science and a poorly understood one. Every arXiv preprint filed under cond-mat.supr-con between September 2021 and September 2026 was harvested month by month, giving 10,303 records, of which 10,049 survive off-topic filtering at a measured bleed rate of 2.5%; a targeted supplement adds 200 records. Each is classified along two axes -- material family and mode of inquiry -- by a language-model pass agreeing with hand labels at 86.3% and 87.0%, per-class recall reported. We then assess 15 of the grid's 45 cells for data availability, benchmark maturity and validation cost. Machine learning has barely entered the field: 1.2% of the seed uses an AI method in its own work. Where it has entered it clusters in ab initio screening and neural quantum states, where a calculation can check a prediction, and in device metrology, where instruments label their own output; we offer validation cost as the hypothesis fitting that pattern, untested against data volume or scientific stakes. Synthesis, the rate-limiting step across families, carries no AI-tagged paper in the seed and one in the merged corpus. The gap between prediction and measurement is concrete: recent screens advertise candidates above 200 K at 200 GPa, while the closed prediction-synthesis-measurement loops found here delivered low-temperature intermetallics, 2.5 to 6.5 K at ambient pressure in the more recent. We conclude that the highest-value interventions are releasing labelled characterization data, building a time-stamped benchmark of experimentally confirmed superconductors, and modelling synthesizability rather than automating synthesis. Corpus, labels and scripts are released.
